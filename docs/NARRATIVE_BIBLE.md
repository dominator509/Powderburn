# POWDERBURN: The Ledger of Elk Creek

## Story promise

POWDERBURN is a historical frontier drama about the cost of turning a private
wrong into public evidence. The player leads the Powderburn Company, a small
band of people who have been used by soldiers, railroads, land offices,
private detectives, and frontier courts. They cross the American West from
1867 to 1878, following the paper trail of Silas Teague and the Elk Creek
Ring.

The company is not trying to conquer the frontier or rewrite its history. The
fixed historical events remain fixed. The company can only decide who is
protected, which evidence survives, what violence it participates in, and
which names are carried into Elias Ward's Ledger.

The emotional engine is the tension between a clean account and a truthful
one. Elias begins by believing that accurate numbers can make a guilty man
useful. Naomi refuses that bargain. Every recruit then tests the same belief
from a different angle: law, labor, land, memory, medicine, or survival.

## Canonical cast

Elias Ward is the company's keeper and the game's moral axis. A former
quartermaster, he signed the false loss report that gave Teague access to
military payroll and land claims. His arc is not redemption by suffering. It
is the slower choice to stop making himself the author of other people's
survival and to let the people named in the Ledger speak.

Naomi Freed is the first ally and the company's practical conscience. Her
brother Josiah was killed after challenging Teague's land theft. Naomi spent
two years assembling the counter-book that connects the Ring's money, riders,
and victims. She joins because evidence without protection is another way to
bury the living. Her destination is Nicodemus: land held by Black settlers,
with no patron's name above the door.

Tsayd-tainte, called Whitehorse, is a Kiowa scout and witness to the gap
between treaty language and lived consequences. Whitehorse never exists to
explain history to the company; he decides when the company has earned the
right to walk beside him. His arc moves from keeping families clear of other
people's wars to carrying names through the machinery of incarceration.

Sergeant Absalom Doyle is a Buffalo Soldier of the 10th Cavalry. He knows
that an order can be lawful, an operation can be successful, and the result
can still be wrong. Doyle is the company's formation, overwatch, and casualty
discipline. His story tests whether duty can become care instead of another
name for obedience.

Wen Li-hsiang is a Chinese railroad worker, powder handler, and witness to
the labor erased from the photographs celebrating the railroad. Wen follows
the money from the grade to the telegraph and finally refuses to let the road
be the only thing that remembers the people who built it.

Cordelia Ames is a former Pinkerton investigator. She has spent years turning
information into a weapon for private clients. She joins when Teague's files
make clear that the agency's dossiers are not neutral records but rented
authority. Her arc is the choice to publish evidence even when it implicates
the institutions that taught her how to gather it.

Hollis Mercer is a former Confederate cavalryman who has polished defeat into
a personal story about honor. The railroad, the hide yard, and the Ledger
strip that story down. Mercer is allowed to become honest, not absolved.

Ignacio Ruelas is a borderlands land and water-rights fighter. He understands
that a courthouse line can cut through a family older than the courthouse.
His arc culminates in the Salt War, where the company must defend community
memory rather than the paper that a stronger party stamped over it.

Tomas Alcantara is a Tejano physician and field surgeon. He has seen the
frontier turn shortages into profit and illness into a weapon. He joins late,
when the company has to decide whether saving lives is still possible after
years of treating the consequences of other people's bargains.

The player-created leader is the tenth voice around the fire. The authored
companions have fixed identities and arcs; the player's Way, skill choices,
survival, and Ledger decisions determine what kind of company Elias is
actually keeping.

## Four-act campaign

### Act I — The Name on the Paper (1867–1868)

The opening act teaches the player that movement is a commitment. Crossings,
cover, smoke, and spacing are not decoration: the company survives by choosing
where to stand before choosing whom to shoot.

1. **Elk Creek: The Debt — April 12, 1867.** Elias and Naomi hold a creek
   crossing, save Naomi, and recover the surviving counter-book pages. The
   first battle is a compact movement lesson: leave the deployment line,
   claim hard cover, and prevent the Ring from reaching the wagon. Naomi joins
   after the battle. The opening Ledger entry is Josiah's absence and Elias's
   signature.
2. **Pawnee Fork: Hancock's Shadow — April 19, 1867.** The company follows a
   Ring courier operating in the wake of Hancock's fixed expedition. The
   player moves between prairie cover and the abandoned camp edge to recover
   proof without treating the historical confrontation as a player victory.
   This is the first lesson in sightlines, wind, and the difference between a
   tactical success and a historical rewrite.
3. **Smoky Hill Station — September 3, 1867.** The company crosses an exposed
   station yard to save Miriam Cole and recover the procurement seals. The
   battlefield is a movement puzzle of mud lanes, station cover, and long
   firing channels. The story moves from one stolen book to a network of
   institutional proof.
4. **Medicine Lodge: Broken Ink — October 21, 1867.** Teague's provocateurs
   try to manufacture a treaty-road attack. Whitehorse joins only if the
   company protects travelers without claiming the treaty gathering as its
   own military story. The tactical identity is restraint under pressure:
   use cover and called shots to stop the Ring's armed crime while keeping the
   company's fire away from the people being used as a pretext.
5. **Washita Winter — November 26, 1868.** The company moves through snow and
   broken visibility at the edge of the fixed Washita event. Whitehorse is a
   required witness. The player learns that smoke, weather, and wounded legs
   change the AP price of movement, and that a route can be safer than a kill.
6. **Washita Aftermath — December 1, 1868.** Doyle joins the winter count's
   escape. The company escorts witnesses through a closing draw while Ring
   scouts try to destroy the list of missing, dead, and taken. This is the
   act's first formation battle: stagger the squad, keep a retreat lane, and
   spend AP on protection rather than chasing the nearest target.

### Act II — The Road Counts Its Builders (1869–1873)

The railroad promises connection while Teague's network turns connection into
leverage. The company's combat language expands from survival to extraction,
interdiction, and the deliberate use of information.

7. **Promontory: The Last Spike — May 10, 1869.** After the famous ceremony,
   Wen identifies saboteurs targeting a payroll car. The company moves along
   railbed and ballast, preserving workers and payroll evidence while the
   historical photograph remains untouched. Wen joins after the mission.
8. **The Rail Grade — June 2, 1869.** Wen's unpaid grade crew is trapped with
   the agency payroll book. The player crosses an open grade, uses elevation
   and hard cover, and breaks the Pinkerton cordon. The story makes labor a
   named stake instead of background scenery.
9. **Denver Extension — June 24, 1870.** Cordelia Ames is cornered in a
   switchyard with deeds showing how Teague bought bankrupt claims. The
   battlefield turns the railroad's geometry against the gunmen: lanes,
   ballast, and a deadline created by the incoming train. Ames joins if the
   company extracts her and the deeds.
10. **Los Angeles Telegram — October 27, 1871.** Wen's contact dies getting a
    decoded telegram out of the wire office. The company must move the message
    across the depot while the Ring closes the exits. Combat is a moving
    handoff: the safest shot is often the one that clears the next tile for
    the courier.
11. **Divide Crossing — August 14, 1872.** Ames's survey party discovers a
    private corridor made from seized homesteads. The company opens a mountain
    pass and recovers the surveyors. Snow, scree, and elevation make the
    squad's movement order matter more than raw accuracy. Hollis Mercer joins
    after the company refuses to turn the survey into another private claim.
12. **Panic of 1873 — September 18, 1873.** The fixed market collapse destroys
    Teague's eastern protection. The company raids the burning office for
    surviving books and reaches the Act II decision. This is the hinge of the
    campaign: evidence can be used to warn people marked for death, or the
    company can enter the hide trade under Teague's badge to reach his network.

The Act II choice is permanent and changes the next mission, not the final
historical record.

### Act III — The Price of a Living Witness (1874–1876)

The Ring no longer hides behind a single false report. It has become a supply
chain. The company now fights inside consequences it helped expose.

13A. **Relief at Adobe Walls — June 26, 1874.** If the company warns the
     witness camp, it reaches the siege before morning. Whitehorse's warning
     becomes a physical extraction route through dust, rubble, and broken
     sightlines. The mission is about bringing people out alive before
     eliminating the Ring gunmen.

13B. **Hide Yard Reckoning — June 26, 1874.** If the company takes the hide
     contract, it enters the yard wearing the Ring's badge. Naomi and Elias
     turn on the overseers, free the captives, and accept that the prisoners
     will remember the disguise. Offal, smoke, and close-range violence make
     this the campaign's most intimate betrayal.

14. **Adobe Walls: Second Morning — June 27, 1874.** The fixed battle remains
    historical. The company fights Teague's separate crime in the confusion,
    preserving evidence while refusing to claim ownership of the battle's
    outcome. Sharps rifles, rubble, elevation, and long sightlines make this
    the first deliberate range-management mission.
15. **The Hide Yard — June 27, 1874.** After the fixed battle, Doyle finds the
    private killing book. The player moves through the stench and smoke to
    seize names and burn prices. The Ledger's moral weight becomes literal
    campaign state: deaths are recorded, not converted into anonymous XP.
16. **Red River Supply — September 12, 1874.** The company cuts a Ring supply
    column without attacking the fixed campaign column. Canyon approaches,
    difficult ground, and black-powder smoke teach the player to create a
    firing pocket, move through it, and close the route behind the enemy.
17. **Black Hills Dispatch — June 29, 1876.** The company receives a dispatch
    four days too late to change the fixed defeat. Whitehorse asks them to
    carry the truth anyway. Flooded ground, rain, and night visibility make
    this a delayed-message movement mission: the objective is meaning, not a
    false rescue of history.

### Act IV — What the Book Cannot Own (1877–1878)

The final act refuses a clean heroic ending. The company can expose the Ring,
save people, and choose solidarity, but it cannot make the decade harmless.
The final question is whether the Ledger is a cage of names or a record that
returns agency to the named.

18. **Great Railroad Strike — July 21, 1877.** Ames recognizes provocateurs
    in the strike files and Doyle sees soldiers preparing to treat workers as
    a formation problem. The company moves through a rain-soaked rail yard,
    separating armed Ring violence from the workers whose strike remains a
    fixed historical event.
19. **Nicodemus — September 17, 1877.** Naomi's destination is a real town
    built by Black settlers. Powderburn defends the settlement's chosen work
    after its fixed founding. The level is open prairie and unfinished homes:
    no fortress, only overlapping fields of fire, evacuation lanes, and the
    knowledge that the company is defending a future rather than a patron.
20. **San Elizario Salt War — December 17, 1877.** Ruelas leads the defense
    of community records after the fixed violence. The company protects memory
    from forged titles and chooses whether to side with Ruelas's community.
    The authored flag `a4_salt_war_sided_with_ruelas` is the moral key that
    opens the final camp and ending.
21. **Fort Marion: Names Carried — April 13, 1878.** Whitehorse and the
    company recover prisoners' names from thieves operating outside the fixed
    incarceration. The fort's walls are a visual contradiction: hard cover
    everywhere, freedom nowhere. The mission asks the player to move names out
    of a system designed to contain them.
22. **Memphis Yellow Fever — September 1, 1878.** Alcantara joins during the
    epidemic. The company recovers stolen quinine and relief funds while the
    fixed epidemic remains immutable. Rain, mud, smoke, and low visibility turn
    medicine into the most important thing on the map. Tomas's arc makes care
    an active battle role rather than a post-battle animation.
23. **Last Account — October 20, 1878.** At camp, the surviving company reads
    the Ledger together. The available conversations depend on who lived and
    how much weight the player allowed the book to carry. The player does not
    select a reward here; they decide what testimony the company is willing to
    keep.
24. **Teague's Counting Room — November 11, 1878.** The company reaches the
    Ring's counting room. Teague wants a duel and a story in which the book is
    Elias's private religion. Elias refuses both. The final battle moves from
    the street to the room where land, labor, silence, and death were priced.
    The company must reach the records, keep the witnesses alive, and end the
    Ring's armed resistance. The ending is determined by the Ledger's living
    names, the chosen Act II branch, Ruelas's flag, and the companions who
    survived.

## Story-to-gameplay contract

Every playable mission must satisfy all of the following:

1. Its campaign-node date and scenario date are identical. Historical fixed
   points cannot drift when a level is regenerated.
2. Its briefing names the human stake, its dialogue names a character's
   current conflict, and its objective uses the same verb as the battlefield
   route: hold, escort, recover, carry, defend, extract, or expose.
3. The map must teach or test one movement idea: cover-to-cover advance,
   crossing control, retreat lane, elevation, difficult terrain, smoke
   management, or a deliberate approach to a protected point.
4. Actor rosters, weapons, and levels must fit the mission's year and the
   campaign's progression. The player's persistent company supplies the
   long-term level curve; authored enemies supply the local tactical identity.
5. A companion's death removes their future dialogue and gates any mission
   that requires their presence. The Ledger records the name before the next
   story beat can begin.
6. A historical fixed event can shape the company's choices, but it cannot be
   replaced by a player-authored victory claim.

The combat kernel remains authoritative for AP, movement legality, line of
sight, hit results, wounds, morale, and death. Narrative text never promises
an outcome the kernel cannot produce. When a mission uses an extraction or
protection verb, the authored actors, route, and post-battle record must make
that stake visible even while the current battle resolution still requires
the Ring force to be broken.

## Endings

The ending is a Ledger outcome, not a binary morality meter.

- **The Empty Account:** the company wins battles but leaves too few living
  witnesses or too little testimony to make Teague's record travel. Elias
  keeps the book and still mistakes possession for justice.
- **The Witness Road:** the company protects people, carries the evidence, and
  accepts that testimony belongs to the people who gave it. The book leaves
  Elias's hands and becomes a chain of living accounts.
- **The Names Carried:** the strongest ending requires the Ruelas branch,
  enough Ledger weight to face the truth, and the survival of the companions
  whose arcs challenge Elias. Teague is exposed, but the final scene makes no
  claim that exposure repairs the country. The company chooses to remember
  without owning the remembered.

There is no ending in which history is erased. The player's authorship is
limited to the company's conduct, the people it protects, and the honesty of
the record it leaves behind.
